Methodology

This site estimates how much of a home's price is due to where it is, separately from what the house itself is like. All code and data pipelines are open; every number can be regenerated from public records.

The price factor

We pool arm's-length residential sales (2023–present) from Charlottesville City and Albemarle County and fit one hedonic regression:

log(price) ~ log(sqft) + beds + baths + age + age² + log(lot acres) + stories + property type + assessor grade + year-quarter + area

The area term is a census-block-group fixed effect (block groups with fewer than 20 sales are pooled up to their tract, and thin tracts to the locality — these are flagged "pooled" in the panel). The price factor shown on the map is the exponentiated area effect, normalized so the sales-weighted geometric mean factor is 1.00 — that is, 1.00 marks the typical location, not the arithmetic average. The headline window starts in 2023 deliberately: pre- and post-pandemic factor maps differ substantially (the city traded at roughly an 18% discount to comparable county homes in 2018–19 and at a slight premium by 2023+, with the largest gains in the city's historically cheapest neighborhoods), so mixing eras would show neither. The time slider under the map modes shows the 2018–19 factor map, fit with the same specification on that era's sales alone. A factor of 1.25 means the same house costs about 25% more there than the metro-wide expectation. The model explains about 88% of the variance in log sale prices (R² = 0.88).

Sample filters: residential use codes (single-family detached/attached, condos), positive prices, county sales marked "Valid Improved" by the assessor, a price-to-assessment sanity band (0.35–3.0) applied to both localities, multi-parcel deeds dropped, and basic sanity bounds on size and price per square foot. Implausible attribute values (a year in the stories column, future construction years) are nulled with a warning at cleaning time rather than silently clipped.

Dollars per month

The reference home price (≈ metro-typical home at factor 1.00) is computed by deflating each of the last 12 months' sales by its area factor and taking the median. Monthly costs are principal + interest on a 30-year fixed mortgage at 6.5% with 20% down. Change any of these assumptions and the levels shift, but the differences between areas — the point of the map — barely move.

All-in cost and the bikeability question

The "Buy: + driving" view adds an estimated commuting cost to the mortgage: straight-line distance from each block group's homes to the nearer of downtown or UVA, driven twice a workday (22 days/month) at $0.70 per mile — an IRS-style all-in cost of car ownership per mile. This is deliberately conservative: one commuter, no other car trips priced in.

The chart marks city-proper block groups green as a proxy for "bikeable." County development areas just outside the city partially connect by greenways, but we treat them as car-dependent; e-bikes stretch the plausible range (shaded ~6 mi band).

Rent

There is no parcel-level public rent data, so rent comes in three layers:

  • Rent: $/mo — ACS 2019–2023 median gross rent (rent + utilities) per block group. Gray where the ACS suppresses the estimate or fewer than 30 renter households live.
  • Rent: factor — observed ACS rent divided by the rent expected from that block group's renter housing stock. The expectation comes from a rent hedonic (bedrooms, single-family share, structure age) fit on ACS PUMS microdata for the two local PUMAs. Caveat: that hedonic explains little of rent variation (R² ≈ 0.08 on ~590 renter households), so this factor mostly reflects raw rent differences — rent varies far less with unit characteristics than sale prices do.
  • Rent: ZORI (ZIP) — Zillow's Observed Rent Index, a market-rate asking-rent index by ZIP code, with year-over-year change. Coarser geography, but current (monthly) where ACS lags several years.

Fine-grained surface

Block groups are the finest unit with enough sales for stable per-area estimates. The optional fine-grained view instead smooths each individual sale's location value (area effect + model residual) onto a 500 m grid with an 800 m Gaussian kernel. Cells whose effective sample is under 8 sales are left blank rather than extrapolated — which is why rural areas show data only where subdivisions exist.

Limitations

  • Sale prices reflect what transacted; areas with few sales (much of the rural county) have wide confidence intervals — click any area to see them.
  • The model controls for what assessors record. Unrecorded quality (renovation level, views, street noise) lands in the area factor when it clusters spatially.
  • Straight-line distance understates real driving distance; commute cost is one commuter only. Both biases make the periphery look cheaper all-in than it really is.
  • ACS block-group rents are 5-year averages (2019–2023) and lag the market.
